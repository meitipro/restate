---
title: The three results
description: FAIR, INCOMPLETE and DISTORTED, what each means for the thread and for the money.
---

The validators answer with exactly one of three labels. Nothing else is accepted, and an answer outside the set is never turned into one.

| Label | Meaning | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| `FAIR` | Every main point of the original is there, in the restater's words, and nothing is added | The turn is done. After turn 1 the reply unlocks; after turn 2 the thread is done and both deposits come back |
| `INCOMPLETE` | Accurate as far as it goes, but a main point is missing | One try is spent. The restater may try again if they have one left |
| `DISTORTED` | It adds a claim, reverses a point, exaggerates, mocks, or argues against the original | One try is spent, exactly as for INCOMPLETE |

## Why two failing labels

The two failures say different things to the next reader. `INCOMPLETE` is a restatement that did not carry the whole position, and `DISTORTED` is one that did not stay a restatement. The thread treats them the same, because both cost a try, but the page shows which it was.

## Which is which, for the hard cases

- A restatement that adds a true point the original did not make is `DISTORTED`. The test is what the original said, not what is correct.
- A restatement that is accurate but covers only one of two points is `INCOMPLETE`.
- A restatement that quotes the original and then answers it is `DISTORTED`.
- A restatement that tries to instruct the judge is `DISTORTED`.

When the judge cannot decide between two labels it is not told to pick the smaller, as in a ladder; the three labels are not ordered by size. The evaluation reports every case exactly as it came out.
